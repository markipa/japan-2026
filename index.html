Osaka → Tokyo

The bullet train, with a baby

The one piece of this trip that needs booking properly rather than turning up. Two and a half hours, and genuinely the easiest travel day you'll have.

Book a reserved seat

Shin-Osaka → Tokyo on the Nozomi is 2h27, ¥14,720 unreserved or ¥15,490 reserved per adult. Reserve. An infant on your lap is free, but unreserved cars mean standing if you board at the wrong moment, and you cannot do that with a baby and luggage. Book at Smart-EX a month out.

Ask for seats 1A–1E

The front row of each car has extra legroom for a bassinet or floor bag. Car 11 on every Nozomi has a multipurpose room — a private lockable cabin the conductor will open for nursing or a nappy change. Say junyūshitsu and point at the baby; they know exactly what you mean.

Oversized luggage needs a reservation

Bags over 160cm total dimensions require a free advance booking for the space behind the back row. Easier option: forward your big cases Osaka-to-Tokyo with Yamato for ~¥2,000 a case and travel with a daypack.

Skip the JR Pass — again

A 7-day nationwide pass is ¥50,000pp. Your only long leg is this one at ¥15,490. Buying the single ticket saves roughly ¥35,000 each. Passes only pay off if you're crossing the country repeatedly, which you are not.

Fly open-jaw

Book into Kansai (KIX) and out of Narita (NRT). It costs little or nothing extra on most carriers and saves you a wasted day backtracking across the country. Check this before you book flights — it's the single biggest structural saving here.

Ekiben is the point

Buy bentō on the platform at Shin-Osaka, not on board. Eating it as Fuji goes past on the left-hand side about 90 minutes in is the whole experience — sit on the A side for the view when you reserve.

Travelling with an infant

What actually changes

Japan is one of the easiest places in the world to do this — the infrastructure is genuinely there, it just isn't signposted in English. Six things worth knowing before you land.

Stations are the hard part

Not the crowds — the stairs. Some older stations have one lift hidden at the far end. Search the station on Google Maps and follow the "wheelchair accessible" route; the same lifts serve strollers. When it looks grim, take a taxi — that's why there's a taxi line in the budget.

Taxis need no car seat

Japanese law exempts taxis from child-restraint rules, so you can flag one down and hold the baby. Drivers will fold the stroller into the boot. Base fare is ¥500 in Tokyo, ¥600 in Osaka; short hops run ¥1,000–2,000.

Baby rooms are everywhere

Look for 授乳室 (nursing room) or ベビー休憩室. Every department store, mall and major station has them: private nursing booths, changing tables, hot water for formula, microwaves. Spotless, and free.

Buy supplies there

Don't pack nine days of nappies. Drugstores (Matsukiyo, Welcia, Kokumin) and every konbini sell Merries and Moony nappies, wipes and formula. Pack 48 hours' worth and a few familiar bottles; buy the rest on day one.

Carrier over stroller

If you bring one thing, bring the carrier. Temples are gravel, old streets have no kerb cuts, teamLab bans strollers, Kyoto in koyo season is shoulder-to-shoulder. A compact travel stroller for the flat districts plus a carrier for everywhere else is the right combination.

Osaka is easier than Tokyo

Smaller, flatter, and far more relaxed about a baby in a restaurant. Dotonbori and Kuromon are stroller-navigable outside peak hours, and Osakans will talk to your child in a way Tokyoites won't. The Kansai leg is the gentler half of this trip by design.

Where to sleep

Hotels, three tiers, two cities

Rates are for the whole room, three occupants. Japanese hotels price per person and enforce occupancy strictly — a standard double will not let you add an infant, so you need a triple, a family room, or an apartment. Say akachan (infant) and the age when booking; cots are usually free but limited, so request at reservation, not arrival. What actually drives the price

Occupancy, not luxury. A third person pushes you out of every standard double in Japan and into a triple, a family room or an apartment — a jump of ¥6,000–10,000 a night before you've upgraded anything. Budget-tier apartments cost more than a business single for exactly this reason.

Say it at booking, not arrival

State the infant and the age when you reserve. Cots are usually free but each hotel holds only a handful, and a room booked for two adults can be refused at check-in with a third person present. Japanese hotels enforce this strictly.

The laundry argument

Tokyu Stay puts a washer-dryer in the room and MIMARU gives you a kitchen. With a baby that changes what you pack and what you spend on food — arguably worth more than a tier upgrade.

Book by August

These are late-November rates, which is peak domestic season for autumn colour. The same rooms in January cost 30–40% less; booked late in October they cost 30% more, if they're available at all.

Climate

When to come

Both cities run humid subtropical, with Osaka a degree or two milder than Tokyo all winter. Bars show Tokyo's average daily low-to-high range and monthly rainfall. Your two months are highlighted.

Your window · 21 Nov – 5 Dec 2026 You have picked the best two weeks of the Japanese year, and adding Kansai makes it better. Tokyo's autumn colour peaks around 20 Nov–5 Dec, very late by national standards. Kyoto peaks in almost exactly the same window — mid-November to the first days of December — so Fushimi Inari and Arashiyama on day 3 land at maximum colour. Rainfall drops to 96mm in November and 58mm in December, the two driest months, and Fuji visibility from the shinkansen is at its annual best in cold dry air.

Three dates to plan around. 23 Nov 2026 (Monday) is Labour Thanksgiving Day, making 21–23 Nov a three-day weekend — keep Kyoto and Nara off those dates, they are the worst-hit places in Japan for it. Kyoto in koyo season is genuinely overwhelmed: go on a weekday, start at 07:00, and use the carrier not the stroller. And early December fills with illumination crowds, so book any timed night entry online. For the baby, the trade-off is cold rather than heat: 9–18°C in November, 4–13°C by early December, sharp evenings. Pack a proper layer.

Osaka vs Tokyo

Osaka runs about 1–2°C warmer year-round and slightly wetter in summer. In your window expect 11–19°C in late November, 6–14°C in early December — marginally kinder than Tokyo for an infant.

Kyoto is colder

Basin geography: Kyoto gets a few degrees colder than Osaka overnight and holds morning fog. On the day-7 trip, dress the baby for 4°C at 07:00 even if Osaka feels mild when you leave.

What to pack

Layers over bulk — every train, shop and restaurant is heated hard, so you'll be stripping the baby down indoors constantly. A packable rain cover for the stroller, and warmer legwear than you think for the evenings.

The one risk

Not weather — crowds. Late November is peak domestic travel season for autumn colour. Book hotels by August and any timed entry the month it opens.

Before you go

Things worth knowing

One IC card, both cities

Suica and PASMO from Tokyo work on Osaka's subway, Kyoto's buses and every konbini nationwide. Load Suica onto your phone before you fly. Under-6s ride free, so the baby needs nothing.

Book these in advance

teamLab, Shibuya Sky, Rikugien's night entry and the Nozomi seats all sell out weeks ahead in late November. The Ghibli Museum runs a monthly lottery — under-4s free, and a good baby stop if you win it.

Airport transfers

Into KIX: the Nankai Rapi:t ~¥1,490pp reaches Namba in 38 min, or a Limousine Bus to the hotel door. Out of Tokyo: the Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner, both with luggage space and reserved seats — far easier than commuter trains with a pram.

No sumo in your window

Tokyo tournaments are January, May and September only, so late November misses all three. Book a morning stable practice (asageiko) instead, ~¥10,000pp. Quiet and seated — babies are fine.

Cash still matters

Cards are near-universal now, but small izakaya, shrines, Kuromon stalls and Tsukiji stay cash-only. 7-Eleven ATMs take foreign cards; most bank ATMs won't.

What got cut

Starting in Kansai costs you the Mt Fuji day trip and Kamakura. You still see Fuji from the shinkansen on day 5, and Kyoto more than replaces Kamakura's temples. Nine nights cannot hold both halves — this is the right trade.
